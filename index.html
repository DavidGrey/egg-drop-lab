How the numbers are worked out

Falling. Gravity pulls with m·g (g = 9.81 m/s²). Air pushes back with drag ½·ρ·Cd·A·v² (air density ρ = 1.225 kg/m³). The egg alone has Cd ≈ 0.47 and is about 4.3 cm wide; a parachute canopy has Cd ≈ 1.5. The fall is stepped forward in 0.5 ms slices to get the speed at impact.

Landing. All of that motion has to stop within the stopping distance d. On a hard floor the shell only gives about 2 mm. A cushion adds the part of its thickness the egg can sink into: foam 85%, bubble wrap 70%, crumpled paper 55%, cotton 90%, balloons 75%. The average force is F = m·v² ÷ (2·d) + m·g.

Balloons. Each balloon is as wide as the cushion is thick. If the force on a balloon passes 30 N it pops. It soaks up some energy first, then the egg hits the floor with whatever speed is left.

Breaking. The egg cracks if F reaches 20 N. This is the average force during the stop; real impacts peak higher, so leave some margin in a real test.

Pictures. The tall view squeezes the whole drop onto the screen, so the egg there is not to scale. The close-up on the right draws the egg and the cushion at their true sizes.
